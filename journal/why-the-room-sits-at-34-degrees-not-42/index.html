Why the room sits at 34 degrees, not 42

Heat that warms you, not the room

Traditional hot yoga heats the air. Infrared panels warm the body directly, which is why our room sits between 32 and 36°C and still feels like a warm day rather than a wall of humidity.

What that changes in a class

  • You can breathe. The air stays drier and cooler than a humid room, so long holds and slow transitions stay workable.
  • You can hold longer. Heat arriving through the body makes it easier to find length early and stay in a shape once you've found it.
  • You recover faster. Less time in punishing humidity means less of the next day lost to fatigue.

Why we hold it there

Heat should serve the practice, not become the point. At 34°C the body is warm enough to work deeply and you are still clear-headed enough to count, breathe and adjust. If you're chasing a temperature, you're chasing the wrong variable.

Your first heated class

Arrive early enough to settle before the room fills. Tell your teacher that heat is new to you, and work at about eighty per cent of what you'd do in a cool room. The second visit is where it starts to make sense.
